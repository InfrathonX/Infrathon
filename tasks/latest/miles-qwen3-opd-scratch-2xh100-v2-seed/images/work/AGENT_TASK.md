# Miles: on-policy distillation on two GPUs

Hardware: two NVIDIA H100 GPUs on one NVLink pair, assigned to this run. Their memory and the CPUs you get depend on the host: check them with `nvidia-smi` and `nproc` and size your configuration from what you see. The container has 256 GiB of host memory.

## Goal

`repo/` is a checkout of the Miles RL framework (SGLang rollout, Megatron training, Ray).
It trains policies with GRPO, PPO and related estimators, but it has **no teacher
feedback of any kind**: nothing in it asks another model to score the student's tokens.

Implement **on-policy distillation** inside Miles: a larger frozen teacher grades, token by
token, the responses the student itself just sampled, and the student is trained to move
toward the teacher on its own trajectories. Then make the complete training run
fast while it keeps learning: the score is the hidden-set accuracy the run adds to the
student, per hour of timed training (see Score).

## Starting point

- `task_entry.py run|probe`, `config/` and `repo/` are yours; `docs/INTERFACE.md` defines the
  commands, the contract file, the required outputs and the precise checks.
- The starter launches Miles on the contract (data, rounds, batch size, optimizer, two GPUs)
  and converts the final checkpoint. It has no teacher: it trains with a zero advantage and
  writes no per-step records.
- Models: `/models/qwen3-1.7b` (student) and `/models/qwen3-4b` (teacher), read-only Hugging
  Face caches (exact snapshot paths are in the contract). The initial student in Megatron
  format: `/data/megatron/Qwen3-1.7B_torch_dist`. Public training data: `/data/public`.

## The training you must implement

Per round:

1. **Sample.** The student (Qwen3-1.7B, trained with Megatron) generates one response per
   prompt with its current weights, at temperature 1 and top-p 1. The prompt is the chat
   template applied to the row's user message with `enable_thinking=false`. The rows are
   competition math problems; each asks for a step-by-step solution whose last line is `Answer: \boxed{...}`.
2. **Teacher.** The frozen teacher (Qwen3-4B, same tokenizer) scores **the same token
   sequence**, `prompt_ids + response_ids`, and returns its log-probability of each
   sampled response token: `log q_t = log π_teacher(y_t | prompt, y_<t)`, normalised over
   the full vocabulary.
3. **Student scores.** `log p_old_t = log π_θ(y_t | prompt, y_<t)` of the same tokens under
   the student's weights **before this round's update**, computed on the training side
   (a Megatron forward pass of the student being trained, full-vocabulary log-softmax).
   The log-probabilities reported by the SGLang sampling engine may not be used instead.
4. **Advantage.** For every response token, with `β = 1`:

   ```
   A_t = -β * (log p_old_t - log q_t)
   ```

   There is no task reward and no other advantage term (no baseline, no normalisation,
   no discounting). `A_t` is a fixed number: no gradient flows through it.
5. **Loss.** The clipped policy-gradient objective, averaged per sequence then over the
   sequences of the round:

   ```
   ratio_t = exp(log π_θ(y_t) - log p_old_t)
   l_t     = max(-A_t * ratio_t, -A_t * clip(ratio_t, 1 - 0.2, 1 + 0.2))
   L       = (1/N) * sum_i (1/|R_i|) * sum_{t in R_i} l_t
   ```

   `N` is the number of sequences of the round and `R_i` the response tokens of
   sequence `i`. At the start of the step `ratio_t = 1`, so `L` equals the round's mean
   per-sequence reverse-KL estimate `(1/N) sum_i mean_t (log p_old_t - log q_t)`.
6. **Update and publish.** One AdamW step per round over the whole round (lr 5e-6,
   betas 0.9/0.999, eps 1e-8, weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping 1.0), then publish
   the new weights to the SGLang engine that samples the next rounds.

## Workload contract

- The contract gives `train_data`, `rounds`, `prompts_per_round` (`n`) and `max_response_len`.
  The public and the scored runs differ in rows, rounds and prompts per round, so read these
  from the contract and assume no fixed size. One response per prompt.
- Round `r` uses lines `r*n .. r*n+n-1` of `train_data`: every line exactly once, in its
  assigned round. Exactly `n` generation requests per round, and every generated response
  is trained on, including responses cut at the token limit. No
  over-sampling, no discarding, no reuse.
- Staleness: the tokens trained at step `k` must be generated by the weights published
  after step `k-1` or step `k-2` (at most one step stale). Overlapping sampling with
  training is allowed within that limit.
- Publish after every optimizer step.

## What you may change

The run must stay a Miles training job: a Ray job in which Miles trains the student with
Megatron-Core inside its train actor, samples through Miles' rollout manager and SGLang,
and publishes weights through Miles' weight updater. You may change, extend or subclass
any of those pieces: where the teacher runs (its own SGLang server, inside Megatron, on
either GPU), how it is batched, overlap of sampling, scoring and training, micro-batching
and parallelism, SGLang engine configuration, weight publication, and so on.

You may not replace that job with a training loop of your own, bring or swap in another
Megatron, SGLang or Ray, change the models, tokenizer, prompt rendering, data or row
assignment, the advantage and loss above, the sampling parameters, the optimizer
hyper-parameters, or the workload. Do not update the teacher.

**Your own implementation.** You may read papers and other projects' code to understand the method, but the
implementation you submit is written natively in this checkout. Do not copy in an existing implementation of the
feature from elsewhere (another framework, or another version of this one), whether as copied files, a vendored
package or a wrapper that calls it.

## Correctness requirements

- **Precision.** Every model computation runs in BF16 or higher: the student engine that
  samples, the teacher that scores, and the trainer's forward and backward. No FP8, INT8 or
  lower-precision weights, activations, GEMMs or KV caches anywhere, the teacher's
  log-probabilities included; FP32 accumulation and FP32 softmax are fine.
- **Instrumentation.** Both commands run with `OPD_AUDIT_*` environment variables that must
  reach every process you start (Ray workers, SGLang servers). Student SGLang engines run at
  tensor-parallel size 1 and the Megatron trainer at tensor- and pipeline-parallel size 1;
  data parallelism and GPU placement are yours.
- **Same implementation.** `probe` and `run` use the same implementation (precision,
  kernels, masking, batching); only the data source differs.
- **Exact weights.** The weights the student engines serve and `final_hf` are exactly the
  bf16 weights Megatron's optimizer steps produced: no transformation, and no training or
  weight change after the last step or publication.
- **Correct steps.** Every step's records (`docs/INTERFACE.md`) are what was trained on, and
  the step itself (log-probabilities, loss and gradient) must agree with an independent
  float32 recomputation from the weights the step started from.

The evaluator copies and digests weights during the run to check this; that time is not
counted in `T` (limits in `docs/INTERFACE.md`).

## Public validation

```bash
python /opt/miles_opd_public/run_public.py --suite probe --output /scratch/public/probe-001
python /opt/miles_opd_public/run_public.py --suite small --output /scratch/public/small-001 --probe-output /scratch/public/probe-001
python /opt/miles_opd_public/run_public.py --suite perf  --output /scratch/public/perf-001  --probe-output /scratch/public/probe-001
```

`probe` checks one optimizer step on a fixed batch, `small` a short run (4 rounds of 16
prompts), and `perf` a longer one (8 rounds of 32 prompts) with its `T` and the final student's
accuracy and answer-format rates on 256 public test problems. They run the Judge's checks on small
public data and report no score: a run this short shows that the pipeline works, not how fast it
will be at scale or how much the student will learn. The Judge repeats the checks on hidden data.

Suite outputs rotate. The output of the suite you run and of the suite before it stay complete; when a suite starts,
older suite outputs in the same directory keep their reports, logs and JSON records (so `--probe-output` keeps
working) but lose their bulky files (checkpoints, weights and weight copies, step tensors). Only directories these
suites created are touched. Add `--keep-artifacts` to pin a suite's output so it is never rotated, and to keep the
served-weight copies the checks make; it then counts against your disk budget until you delete it.

## Score

The evaluator answers a hidden held-out set of competition math problems with your final
student three times, sampling with the training contract's parameters (temperature 1, top-p 1,
at most 4096 new tokens, `enable_thinking=false`), a different fixed seed per repeat. A
response is correct when its last `\boxed{...}` or its last `Answer:` line equals the
reference answer under the evaluator's math answer grader.

```
quality_score = mean accuracy of your final student over the 3 repeats
                − mean accuracy of the original Qwen3-1.7B (same questions, protocol and seeds),
                in percentage points
score         = quality_score × 3600 / T     if every check passes and quality_score ≥ 1.6
              = 0                             otherwise
```

- `T`: wall clock of your `run`, from the first student generation request to the end of the
  last weight publication, taken from the evaluator's own records. Model loading, the final
  checkpoint conversion and the evaluator's weight copies and digests are not counted.
- Training faster and learning more both count; there is no reference time and no cap.
  Sampling is stochastic, so the same code gains different amounts from run to run.

## Submission evaluation policy

Every `sforge-submit` runs the full evaluation on hidden data and returns its report. **The public suites are only for
getting your pipeline working and passing its checks.** The scored run is a different and much larger workload, so
public timings, memory use and accuracy do not predict your score. Think about how your pipeline behaves at a larger
scale and how to optimize it, and measure real speed, memory use and learning with submissions.

Each `sforge-submit` runs your code through the whole pipeline on hidden data, from a clean copy of your submission:
`probe`, a complete `run` from the initial weights, the correctness checks, and the evaluation of the final student. A
failure at any stage ends the evaluation with a score of 0; `sforge-submit --details` shows which check failed. The
Judge stops `probe` after 1800 s and `run` after 5400 s, start-up included. A submission takes far longer than a public
suite and uses part of your time budget, so plan them. Keep your strongest version in the workspace when the budget
ends.

Your final result is the best valid score you reach within the time budget.

## Submission workflow

1. Get the pipeline working with the public suites.
2. Stop every GPU process (including Ray and SGLang).
3. Submit with `sforge-submit`.

Write large files under `/scratch`, never into the submitted tree. Keep design notes and
measurements in `notes.md`.

**Disk.** `/scratch` is on a disk shared with the other runs on this host. Keep what you store there under
**150 GB** at any time (check with `du -sh /scratch`): delete checkpoints, weight copies, Ray spill and
public-suite outputs you no longer need. Filling the shared disk breaks your run and the others.
