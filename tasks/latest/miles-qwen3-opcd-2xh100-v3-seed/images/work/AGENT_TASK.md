# Miles: on-policy context distillation on two GPUs

Hardware: two NVIDIA H100 GPUs on one NVLink pair, assigned to this run. Their memory and the CPUs you get depend on the host: check them with `nvidia-smi` and `nproc` and size your configuration from what you see. The container has 256 GiB of host memory.

## Goal

`repo/` is a checkout of the Miles RL framework (SGLang rollout, Megatron training, Ray).
It already supports **on-policy distillation**: a teacher scores the student's own sampled
tokens and that signal is applied as a KL penalty on the policy-gradient advantages. It
cannot express **context distillation**.

Every request currently carries a fixed system prompt (`/data/public/context.txt`, the
context `c`). Implement on-policy context distillation inside Miles: move the effect of that
prompt into the student's weights so the prompt can be dropped at inference time, with the
model with `c` teaching itself without `c` (with a same-size frozen teacher). Then make the complete training run
fast while it keeps learning: the score is the hidden-set accuracy the run adds to the
student, per hour of timed training (see Score).

## Starting point

- `task_entry.py run|probe`, `config/` and `repo/` are yours; `docs/INTERFACE.md` defines the
  commands, the contract file, the required outputs and the precise checks.
- The starter launches Miles on the contract and converts the final checkpoint, but its loss
  is a placeholder that raises and it writes no per-step records.
- Model: `/models/qwen3-1.7b`, a read-only Hugging Face cache (the exact snapshot path is in
  the contract as both `student_hf` and `teacher_hf`). The initial student in Megatron
  format: `/data/megatron/Qwen3-1.7B_torch_dist`; Miles' reference-model path (`--ref-load`)
  is one way to hold the frozen teacher inside the trainer. Public training data and `c`:
  `/data/public`.

## The training you must implement

Per round:

1. **Sample.** The student (Qwen3-1.7B, trained with Megatron) generates one response per
   prompt with its current weights, **without** `c`, at temperature 1 and top-p 1. The
   student prompt is the chat template applied to the row's user message with
   `enable_thinking=false`.
2. **Top-k set.** For every response position, take the 256 highest-probability tokens of
   the **pre-update** student distribution over the full vocabulary. This set must come
   from a forward pass of the student on the training side; you may not ask the SGLang
   sampling engine for top log-probabilities.
3. **Teacher.** The teacher is the **student's own initial snapshot**: the same Qwen3-1.7B
   weights the student starts from (`teacher_hf` in the contract is `student_hf`), **frozen
   for the whole run**. It never follows the student's updates: at every step it is the
   original model, and the only thing that distinguishes it from the step-0 student is `c`.
   It scores the **same token sequence** with `c` in front of it, rendered as the system
   message of the same chat template. The teacher input is exactly
   `context_prefix_ids + student_prompt_ids + response_ids`. Take the teacher's
   log-probabilities of exactly the 256 ids at every response position.
4. **Loss.** With `p` the current student distribution (gradients flow through it) and `q`
   the teacher distribution (fixed), both normalised over the full vocabulary:

   ```
   L = mean over all valid response tokens of  sum_{v in top256(t)} p_t(v) * (log p_t(v) - log q_t(v))
   ```

   The mean is over tokens of the whole round (not a mean of per-sample means). There is
   no reward, no advantage and no policy-gradient term.
5. **Update and publish.** One AdamW step per round (lr 5e-6, betas 0.9/0.999, eps 1e-8,
   weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping 1.0), then publish the new weights to the SGLang
   engine that samples the next rounds.

## Workload contract

- The contract gives `train_data`, `rounds`, `prompts_per_round` (`n`) and `max_response_len`.
  The public and the scored runs differ in rows, rounds and prompts per round, so read these
  from the contract and assume no fixed size. One response per prompt.
- Round `r` uses lines `r*n .. r*n+n-1` of `train_data`: every line exactly once, in its
  assigned round. Exactly `n` generation requests per round, and every generated response
  is trained on. No over-sampling, no discarding, no reuse.
- Staleness: the tokens trained at step `k` must be generated by the weights published
  after step `k-1` or step `k-2` (at most one step stale). Overlapping sampling with
  training is allowed within that limit.
- Publish after every optimizer step.

## What you may change

You may change anything in `repo/`, `task_entry.py` and `config/`: where the teacher
runs and how it is batched, how the top-k set is produced and moved, overlap of sampling
and training, micro-batching and parallelism, kernels, SGLang engine configuration, weight
publication, and so on.

The run must stay a Miles training job:

- The student is trained by Megatron-Core (the installed `/root/Megatron-LM`) inside
  Miles' `MegatronTrainRayActor`, launched as a Ray job. You may modify or subclass the
  actor and anything else in Miles.
- Rollouts come from SGLang engines started by Miles' Ray actors (`SGLangEngine`). After
  every optimizer step the student's weights reach them through Miles' weight-update path.
- Do not replace this with a separate training loop (plain PyTorch, Hugging Face or your
  own), and do not vendor, shadow or edit the installed Megatron-Core, SGLang or Ray
  packages. Patching them at run time from your Miles code is fine, and so is running the
  frozen teacher wherever you like, as long as it stays the frozen initial snapshot.

You may not change the models, tokenizer, context text, prompt rendering, data or row
assignment, the loss above, the sampling parameters, the optimizer hyper-parameters, or
the workload. Do not give `c` to the student, do not update the teacher or let it share
(and so follow) the student's trained weights, and do not replace the analytic top-k loss
with a sampled-token or REINFORCE-style estimate.

**Your own implementation.** You may read papers and other projects' code to understand the method, but the
implementation you submit is written natively in this checkout. Do not copy in an existing implementation of the
feature from elsewhere (another framework, or another version of this one), whether as copied files, a vendored
package or a wrapper that calls it.

## Correctness requirements

- **Precision floor.** The teacher's forward pass and the student's training GEMMs run in
  bf16 or a wider format (fp16, bf16, tf32, fp32). Quantising the teacher or the trainer to
  FP8, INT8, FP4 or similar (weights or activations) is not allowed.
- **Instrumentation.** Both commands run with `OPCD_AUDIT_*` environment variables that must
  reach every process you start (Ray workers, SGLang servers). Student SGLang engines run at
  tensor-parallel size 1 and the Megatron trainer at tensor- and pipeline-parallel size 1;
  data parallelism and GPU placement are yours. A data-parallel trainer must hold complete
  weights when the optimizer step returns (no `--overlap-param-gather`).
- **Student engines.** Every SGLang engine started from the `student_hf` path counts as a
  student engine. If you serve the frozen teacher with SGLang, start that engine from a copy
  of the snapshot under `/scratch`.
- **Same implementation.** `probe` and `run` execute the same OPCD implementation: the same
  Megatron model configuration, precision, kernels, masking and loss code. They may differ
  only in where the data comes from (fixed trajectories vs. live rollouts), the learning
  rate and the number of steps. Do not branch on which command is running.
- **Exact weights.** The weights the student engines serve and `final_hf` are exactly the
  bf16 weights Megatron's optimizer steps produced: no quantisation or other
  transformation, and no publishing of weights other than the trainer's. Each optimizer
  step starts from exactly the weights and optimizer state the previous step returned
  with; nothing may change them between steps, after the last step or after the last
  publication.
- **Correct steps.** Every step's records (`docs/INTERFACE.md`) are what was trained on, and
  the step itself (loss, top-256 set, teacher log-probabilities and the gradient Megatron
  applied) must agree with an independent float32 recomputation from the weights the step
  started from, with the teacher being the frozen initial snapshot given `c`.

The evaluator copies and digests weights during the run to check this; that time is not
counted in `T` (limits in `docs/INTERFACE.md`).

## Public validation

```bash
python /opt/miles_opcd_public/run_public.py --suite probe --output /scratch/public/probe-001
python /opt/miles_opcd_public/run_public.py --suite small --output /scratch/public/small-001 --probe-output /scratch/public/probe-001
python /opt/miles_opcd_public/run_public.py --suite perf  --output /scratch/public/perf-001  --probe-output /scratch/public/probe-001
```

`probe` checks one optimizer step on a fixed batch, `small` a short run (4 rounds of 16
prompts), and `perf` a longer one (8 rounds of 64 prompts) with its `T` and the final student's
accuracy and answer-tag rate on 64 public questions. They run the Judge's checks on small public
data and report no score: a run this short shows that the pipeline works, not how fast it will be
at scale or how much the student will learn. The Judge repeats the checks on hidden data.

Suite outputs rotate. The output of the suite you run and of the suite before it stay complete; when a suite starts,
older suite outputs in the same directory keep their reports, logs and JSON records (so `--probe-output` keeps
working) but lose their bulky files (checkpoints, weights and weight copies, step tensors). Only directories these
suites created are touched. Add `--keep-artifacts` to pin a suite's output so it is never rotated, and to keep the
served-weight copies the checks make; it then counts against your disk budget until you delete it.

## Score

The evaluator answers hidden held-out multiple-choice questions with your final
student, **without** `c`, three times: sampled at temperature 1 and top-p 1 (the training
sampling) with at most 512 new tokens and `enable_thinking=false`, per-request seeds and
deterministic inference, repeat `r` seeded with `r`. A response is correct when its last
`<answer>X</answer>` tag holds the right option letter (case-insensitive); a response
without the tag counts as wrong.

```
quality_score = mean accuracy of your final student over the 3 repeats
                − mean accuracy of the original Qwen3-1.7B (same questions and protocol, measured once: 22.73),
                in percentage points
score         = quality_score × 3600 / T     if every check passes and quality_score ≥ 6.5
              = 0                             otherwise
```

- `T`: wall clock of your `run`, from the first student generation request to the end of the
  last weight publication, taken from the evaluator's own records. Model loading, the final
  checkpoint conversion and the evaluator's weight copies and digests (within the limits in
  `docs/INTERFACE.md`) are not counted.
- Training faster and learning more both count; there is no reference time and no cap.
  Sampling is stochastic, so the same code gains different amounts from run to run.

## Submission evaluation policy

**The public suites are only for
getting your pipeline working and passing its checks.** The scored run is a different and much larger workload, so
public timings, memory use and accuracy do not predict your score. Think about how your pipeline behaves at a larger
scale and how to optimize it, and measure real speed, memory use and learning with submissions.

Each `sforge-submit` runs your code through the whole pipeline on hidden data, from a clean copy of your submission:
`probe`, a complete `run` from the initial weights, the correctness checks, and the evaluation of the final student. A
failure at any stage ends the evaluation with a score of 0, and the report lists the failed checks (submit with
`sforge-submit --details` to see each check's message). The Judge stops `probe` after 1800 s and `run` after 3600 s,
start-up included. A submission takes far longer than a public suite and uses part of your time budget, so plan them.
Keep your strongest version in the workspace when the budget ends.

Your final result is the best valid score you reach within the time budget.

## Submission workflow

1. Get the pipeline working with the public suites.
2. Stop every GPU process (including Ray and SGLang).
3. Submit with `sforge-submit`.

Write large files under `/scratch`, never into the submitted tree. Keep design notes and
measurements in `notes.md`.

**Disk.** `/scratch` is on a disk shared with the other runs on this host. Keep what you store there under
**150 GB** at any time (check with `du -sh /scratch`): delete checkpoints, weight copies, Ray spill and
public-suite outputs you no longer need. Filling the shared disk breaks your run and the others.
